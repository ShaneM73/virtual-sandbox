Virtual AR Sandbox

Designed by Xin Miao · Missouri State University

The Augmented Reality Sandbox, moved from a physical box of sand into a virtual 3D world. It runs in a web browser on an ordinary Windows computer, or in VR on a Meta Quest, so any student can shape a landscape and watch water flow over it.

The idea

The physical AR Sandbox is one of the best tools there is for teaching topographic maps, contour lines and watersheds. Building one, though, takes a sandbox, a depth camera, a projector, a Linux computer and a careful calibration. Most students only get to use one if their campus or a nearby museum has it.

This project keeps what makes the sandbox work and drops the hardware. The water still runs on the original SARndbox simulation, ported unchanged in its physics. The sand is virtual, the projector is the screen or the headset, and everything runs in a web browser on Windows, the system most students already have.

History of the physical AR Sandbox

  1. Origin
    The sparkA video of “Sandy Station,” a prototype by Czech researchers that colored sand by elevation and added simple water flow, inspired the UC Davis team.
  2. 2012
    Built at UC DavisOliver Kreylos at the W.M. Keck Center for Active Visualization in the Earth Sciences (KeckCAVES) develops the AR Sandbox. It is part of LakeViz3D, an NSF-funded informal science education project on freshwater lakes and watersheds (grants 1114663 and 1135588).
  3. 2012
    First exhibitsThe first sandboxes open at UC Davis, the Lawrence Hall of Science in Berkeley, the UC Davis Tahoe Environmental Research Center, and the ECHO Lake Aquarium and Science Center in Vermont.
  4. 2012–2021
    Open-source softwareSARndbox is released under the GNU GPL. It runs on Linux with the Vrui VR toolkit and a Kinect camera. Later versions add support for the Kinect v2 and Intel RealSense cameras (2.4) and remote streaming (2.7).
  5. 2021
    SARndbox 2.8Released March 9, 2021. It's the version this project's water simulation is ported from.
  6. 2022
    Community forumA user support forum opens for the many groups building their own sandboxes.
  7. Since then
    WorldwideMore than 150 universities, research centers, government agencies and schools have adopted the AR Sandbox for teaching topography, hydrology and geology, according to Eos.
  8. 2026
    Virtual AR SandboxXin Miao moves the sandbox into a virtual 3D environment that runs in a browser on Windows and in VR on Meta Quest, so students can use it without the special hardware.
